RND.

A co-op horror game where a team of scientists goes into places where unknown creatures live to research and test them, and tries to get out alive.

This page is the plain-English version of where the project stands, so everyone is working from the same picture. Every idea carries a tag:

Decided locked in Leaning probably, not locked Idea on the table Open needs an answer

Working on now

In progress · 25 Sep 2026

More test chambers for the maze run. A run used to be the same room three times. There are now three chambers, and a run plays them from easiest to hardest:

  1. Crates. The exit opens while its plate is held down, and there's no heavy case this time. Four crates side by side are heavy enough; a jar or three crates aren't.
  2. The closet. The first chamber we built: the heavy case is shut in a closet behind a button door.
  3. Ledge. The exit needs its plate held and a button pressed that sits on a ledge 3.5 m up, out of reach. Hit it with a thrown jar, then everyone has 5 s to get through, or you jam the door open with a crate.

Still to do here: playtest the new chambers (can you spot the ledge button, and hit it?), build more chambers so runs vary, and later start the difficulty from the party's level.

Then: the rest of the maze run. A finished run pays out a reward, and you get somewhere to spend it between runs.

The game in one minute

Think R.E.P.O.: you and your friends carry fragile things through a dangerous place using real physics, and it gets chaotic. On top of that we add a gas mask you see the whole game through, a filter that runs out, and a creature that hunts you by sight and sound.

You're there to research and test: collect data and specimens, run tests, and bring them back. Between rounds, that research turns into upgrades and a research tree. What exactly research earns you, and whether selling it (to the government or the black market) is part of the game, is Open.

Our one rule Decided: make everything as smart and reactive as possible. Creatures react to what's actually happening instead of following a script. If it's a choice between a simple scripted trick and something that genuinely reacts, we pick the reactive one and fix its rough edges.

Inspired by R.E.P.O. (carrying, co-op chaos), Portal (test chambers, physics puzzles), Phasmophobia (prestige), Factorio (research tree) and crate opening.

The secret

Team only · the player is never told this

The scientists are rats. Each one is a rat with a human brain uploaded into it. The real scientists are testing whether humans in rat bodies beat ordinary rats, which is why the game has different modes: they're different test environments. Idea

Clues, never explanations Decided. No text, announcer or HUD ever states the twist. Players work it out from things they notice: the huge observation window, teammates flickering into rats, oddly tall door frames.

The mask is the lie Idea. The "filter" doesn't clean the air. It feeds you a gas that keeps up the illusion that you're human. When it runs low, the truth leaks in: a friend looks like a rat for a moment, voices squeak. Everyone sees this separately, based on their own filter. Seeing the truth might even be useful (rat-sized gaps, scent trails), so players may choose to let it run down.

Why you keep upgrades when you die Leaning. The real you is outside the test. When the rat dies, you don't, so you keep what's "in your head" (research, upgrades, level) and lose what the rat carried (gear, loot).

How a round works

The retrieval mode, the one most like R.E.P.O. Leaning

  1. Deploy into a site where something lives.
  2. Research and test: collect data and specimens and run tests while dealing with the creatures.
  3. Get out alive with what you carried.
  4. Hand in the research. What it earns, and whether selling is part of it, is still open.
  5. Upgrade between rounds: research tree, upgrades, crate opening. Never mid-round.

Your characters last Leaning. Unlike R.E.P.O., characters are saved between sessions. The twist: a character you don't play gets rusty. "Time" means runs played on your other characters, not days on the calendar, so taking a break never hurts you. How characters are saved is Decided; which abilities decay and by how much is still open.

Game modes

Retrieval might be one of several modes Idea: retrieval, maze solving, payload delivery and research. Only the maze has been designed so far.

The maze: Portal-style test chambers

A maze is a chain of test chambers. Each one is a physics puzzle: get the whole team from the entry door to the exit. Clean white-and-grey rooms, bright light, and a big observation window high on the wall where the real scientists watch.

  • Pressure plates held down by weight: a player, the heavy case, or a pile of crates. A jar isn't heavy enough. Built.
  • Timed buttons you press, or hit with a thrown crate. The door stays open for 5 seconds, so someone has to run. Built.
  • Doors never crush anyone. Something in the doorway stops them, so a crate can jam a door open. Improvising is the point. Built.
  • Co-op locks, acid-melted panels, sound-triggered doors Idea
  • The experimenters adapt. Solve too fast and the next chamber gets harder; struggle and they "help" in a condescending way. They talk over the PA, getting stranger as your mind slips. Idea
  • No "you beat the rat" score Decided. It would give the secret away.
  • Rooms are stitched together each round from a pool, getting harder the further in you go. Leaning
  • Starting difficulty leans toward the strongest player, so a strong player can't be carried and nobody can game it with low-level friends. Leaning
  • No portal gun for now. Leaning
  • The evil guy is let loose 20 seconds into every chamber, with a growl everyone hears. A shut door stops him; an open one doesn't. Built.
  • A run is three chambers, easiest first. Built, with three chambers so far.

What's playable today

Two ways to play, picked by the host in the menu: a dark test room with a creature in it, and a maze run through three grey-box test chambers. Up to a few players on the same network.

Carrying

  • Grab, carry, fling and throw crates, jars and a heavy case
  • Heavy things feel heavy
  • Push and pull what you're holding

The gas mask

  • You see everything through a full-face mask
  • Cracks in the glass are your health. Hits crack the side they came from
  • The HUD glows green on the glass
  • Breath fogs the glass, the mask sways

The filter

  • Lasts 180 s, faster if you sprint
  • Below 15% your mind starts to go: the HUD scrambles and tears, vision narrows, ears ring
  • Spare canisters to screw on, carry or throw to a friend

Sound

  • You hear yourself breathe, wheeze as the filter wears, and a heartbeat when badly hurt
  • The world sounds muffled through the mask, clearer as it cracks
  • All sounds are generated by the game (placeholders for now)

The evil guy

  • Our first creature (placeholder look: a tall dark shape, red eyes)
  • Only knows what it sees and hears. Sprinting is loud, walking is quiet
  • Eyes show its mood: dim, orange (suspicious), bright red (hunting)
  • Searches where it last saw you, then gives up
  • Always walks a real path to you. You can outrun it by sprinting

Fighting & dying

  • The Scientist throws a flask of glowing acid: 50 damage, 2 m splash, refills in 5 s
  • You have 100 health; it hits for 20
  • In the test room, dead players respawn after 8 s (placeholder)
  • In the maze you stay down until a teammate revives you

Look: dark comic-book style with hard-edged light and thin ink outlines, plus a grainy cheap-camera filter. Still being judged Leaning; press F2 in-game to compare with and without.

Known rough edges

On purpose for now, so don't file these as bugs:

  • You can only play together on the same network. Online play comes with Steam.
  • Names above players show numbers, not names (also comes with Steam).
  • Walking into props doesn't push them; you have to grab them.
  • No crouching or stamina yet.
  • Death is a placeholder: you tip over. In the test room you come back after 8 seconds.
  • Only one creature type, and it can drop down ledges but can't jump up.
  • Sounds are placeholders, tuned by numbers rather than by ear.
  • Finishing a maze run earns nothing yet: after the result screen, a new run starts.
  • Every run plays the same three chambers in the same order, and the two new ones haven't been playtested.
  • The chamber's plates, buttons and doors haven't been tested with several players yet, and they don't have their own sounds.
  • If the game acts strangely right after pulling new code, close Godot completely and reopen it.

How it's built

  • Godot 4.7 (.NET edition) with C#. Everyone must use the same Godot version.
  • One player hosts and everyone else joins them. No servers to run. The host is in charge of anything shared (props, health, the creature) so nobody's game disagrees; your own movement is handled on your machine so it feels instant.
  • Jolt physics, because stacking and carrying is the heart of the game.
  • All sound is generated in code. There are no audio files yet.
  • Steam, voice chat and our own server are planned but not chosen.
  • Automated tests start the game without a window and check that levels load, combat works and two players can connect.

Setup steps are in the repo's README.

For artists

  • Format: .glb, real-world size (1 unit = 1 metre), origin at the bottom centre.
  • Size budget: small props 500–3,000 triangles, characters 5–15k.
  • Name every part (Glass, Liquid, Cap, Label). The code finds parts by name.
  • No painted lighting or shadows in textures. Flat colour areas and clean, deliberate sharp and smooth edges. The game does the shading.
  • Anything that changes during play is code. Model the thing, not the effect. For liquids, model a closed Liquid shape filling the whole inside; the game sets how full it is and makes it slosh.
  • No collision shapes needed; we make those in Godot.
  • Nothing is rigged or animated yet. When it is, each animation gets a fixed name (Idle, Walk, Run…) that the code plays.

Glossary

The evil guy
Our first and only creature, a placeholder.
Filter
The canister on your mask. Runs out. Secretly not a filter.
Mental damage
Damage to your mind rather than your body. Scrambles the HUD instead of cracking the glass, and can't kill you.
Psychosis
Working name for seeing the truth: you and your friends look like rats.
Test chamber
One puzzle room in the maze mode.
Maze run
Three test chambers in a row, easiest first. Pass them all to pass the run; if everyone is down at once, the run fails.
Kit
The items a scientist type carries in hotbar slots 2–5.
Host
The player whose game everyone else joins.
Grey-box
A level made of plain blocks to test gameplay before art.
